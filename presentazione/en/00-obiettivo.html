Where it comes from

The goal of the platform

The starting point is not a game but a question: what do today's tablet games for autistic children aged 3–8 lack?

To make a small set of games enjoyable, accessible and adaptable, while reducing the work of whoever sets them up and linking them to the child's daily life.

A few games, well adapted

There are already many apps. The opportunity is not to add exercises to a catalogue, but to make a few games adapt to each child.

The way changes, not the task

If the child chooses well but dragging fails, the game switches to two taps: same objects, same right answer. The system proposes the change after the session, citing the data («right choice 9 times out of 10, drag failed 6 times»), but it only happens if the adult accepts.

From tablet to real life

On the tablet the child puts photos of their objects into a drawn backpack, but doing it there is not doing it at home. After the game the adult can have the child pack the real backpack and note how it went: the two results are read side by side, never added up.

The platform keeps three things apart

The game rules

What the game asks and how the child answers. «Pack the backpack» is the first: the prototype on which to test the ideas, one at a time.

The materials

The adult photographs the child's real objects (their notebook, their pencil case) and a family member records the instruction. The child then sees those photos and hears that voice, not drawings and a synthetic voice. Everything stays on the tablet.

The profile

What the adult has confirmed for that child, for example «answers with two taps». It is kept apart from the system's estimates, and will avoid starting from scratch in another game.
