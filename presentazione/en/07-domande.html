What we want to test

Five questions. None of them has been tested with children yet.

QuestionWhat we observeWhat we will not be able to conclude
1 · Access. Does changing the gesture (two taps, a more tolerant backpack) let the child show they can choose, without changing the task?independent trials and failed gestures, before and after the changethat the change caused the improvement: replications are needed (Horner et al., 2005)
2 · Variations. Does alternating positions and photos help tell whether the child recognizes the object, and not a place or a picture?performance by position, photo and prompt, with its uncertainty; pauses and exitsthat recognizing a new photo means recognizing the real object
3 · Real backpack. What happens with the real objects, and is the adult's note useful?real outcomes next to game outcomes, never added togetherthat the game produced the transfer
4 · Preferences. Does a confirmed preference also hold in a second game?whether preferences are applied, and whether the system declares those it cannot honorthat a skill carries over from one game to another
5 · The engine. Do the system's proposals do better than an adult setting everything by hand, or a simple rule?the comparison on the next pagesuperiority over the Pickstar product

For each question we decide in advance what would change our mind. For example: if the «picks well but cannot drag» case does not occur among the children followed, the idea must be rethought.
